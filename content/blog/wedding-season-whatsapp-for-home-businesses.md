For home-run businesses selling outfits, jewellery, cakes, mehendi, décor or gifts, wedding season is make-or-break. Orders spike, DMs pile up across Instagram and WhatsApp, and you're packing parcels at midnight. A little setup now means you can say yes to more orders without losing your sleep, or your customers.

## The wedding-season problem

The same pattern shows up for almost every home business:

- The same questions over and over: price, sizes, delivery date, customisation
- Customers who message, go quiet and never come back
- Payments to chase and orders to track across chats and notebooks
- No time left to actually make and ship the products

WhatsApp is where most of these conversations end up, so that's where to fix them.

## 1. Move Instagram enquiries to WhatsApp

Put a **wa.me link** in your Instagram bio and story highlights with a pre-filled message like "Hi! I saw your bridal collection." Add a **QR code** to your packaging and cards for repeat orders. Everything lands in one place, instead of scattered DMs.

## 2. Set an instant reply with your essentials

A welcome flow answers the moment someone messages: your current collection or menu, price range, delivery timelines and a few buttons like **See collection**, **Custom order** and **Delivery dates**. Customers get answers in seconds, and you only step in when they're ready to order.

:::tip
In peak weeks, be upfront about timelines. A message like "Orders placed this week deliver by 18 Dec" prevents disappointment and saves you a dozen "when will it arrive?" chats.
:::

## 3. Collect order details in one go

Use a flow or **WhatsApp Form** to collect size, colour, quantity, delivery date and address, instead of asking one question at a time across a long chat. Tag each contact with what they ordered so you can find them later.

## 4. Take payment before you start making

Send a **payment link** or use **WhatsApp Pay** so customers can pay an advance by UPI right in the chat. Confirm the order automatically once it's paid, and stop chasing screenshots.

## 5. Protect your evenings with business hours

Add a **Business Hours** block to your welcome flow. Outside your hours, it tells customers when you'll be back and still offers the menu, so they get answers while you rest. During your hours, chats can go straight to you.

## 6. Keep customers updated

Send a quick update when an order is ready, dispatched or out for delivery. If you track orders in a Google Sheet, the [Google Sheets integration](/integrations/google-sheets) can send these automatically when you change the status column.

## 7. Turn this season's buyers into next season's

After delivery, thank customers and ask for a photo or review. Then, with their permission, send occasional broadcasts for new collections, festive offers or restocks. Wedding guests become birthday, anniversary and festival customers.
