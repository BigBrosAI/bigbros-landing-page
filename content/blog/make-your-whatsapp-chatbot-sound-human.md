Customers can tell within one message whether they're talking to something helpful or something robotic. "Your query has been registered" makes people want to throw their phone. "Got it! Let me check that for you" makes them stay. The good news: making a chatbot sound human is mostly about writing, not technology.

## Start with a one-page voice card

Before you write a single flow, decide how your brand sounds. Four short answers are enough:

![A chatbot voice card with four sections: how we sound, what we say, what we avoid and when we hand over](/images/blog/make-your-whatsapp-chatbot-sound-human/voice-card.svg "Pin this next to whoever writes your flows.")

- **How we sound:** warm, quick, helpful, like your best staff member on a good day
- **What we say:** short sentences, the customer's name, phrases like "Got it" and "Happy to help"
- **What we avoid:** "Kindly", "Your query", jargon, long paragraphs, ALL CAPS
- **When we hand over:** when someone is upset, confused, or about to place a big order

## Write like you talk

Read every message out loud. If you wouldn't say it to a customer at your counter, rewrite it.

| Robotic | Human |
| --- | --- |
| Please select an option from the menu below. | What can I help you with? |
| Your appointment request has been received. | You're booked! See you Saturday at 11:30. |
| Invalid input. Please try again. | Sorry, I didn't catch that. Tap one of the buttons below. |

## Keep messages short

On a phone screen, three short messages beat one long one. Aim for one idea per message and one question at a time. Use buttons for choices so customers can answer with a tap.

:::tip
Emojis are fine in moderation if they fit your brand. One per message is plenty, and skip them in sensitive conversations like complaints or medical questions.
:::

## Use the customer's context

Save answers into variables and use them later in the conversation: "Great, Priya, I've noted Saturday." Remembering what someone just told you is the simplest way to sound human.

## Know when to step aside

The most human thing a bot can do is admit it's out of its depth. Add a **Human Handoff** block for complaints, complex questions and high-value orders. The conversation moves to your team in the shared inbox with the full history, so the customer never has to repeat themselves.

## Review real conversations every week

Read a handful of bot conversations each week. Where did people get stuck? Where did they ask for a human? Rewrite those messages. Small, regular edits make a bigger difference than a perfect launch.
